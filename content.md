# _Garden City_: Work, Rest & Tech Culture

John Mark Comer's _Garden City_ reframes our 9-to-5 as part of God's grand story from Eden to the New Creation. Comer takes us **"from creation to the final heavenly city"**, helping readers find their place in God's plan[\[1\]](https://www.zondervan.com/9780310337317/garden-city/#:~:text=,changing%20ideas). In Genesis, humanity is made in God's image with a mandate to "take all the raw materials…to work it, to take care of it…as an act of service and worship"[\[2\]](https://navmissionalenterprise.org/garden-city/#:~:text=,the%20God%20who%20made%20you). In other words, even coding or system administration is sacred: our tech work becomes _worship_ when we use it to care for God's world. The biblical narrative continues through the Fall (which makes work "hard" and frustrating) and climaxes with Christ and the New Jerusalem. In the _Garden City_ vision, the future is not a return to Eden but a forward journey into a "Garden-like world" where we help "cooperate with God in building a civilization" for God's people to flourish[\[3\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=You%E2%80%99ll%20hear%20a%20few%20John,a%20civilization%20where%20his%20people). In fact, Becky McKemey notes, in our resurrected lives "our work will be a joy, and not a toil or a burden"[\[4\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=The%20final%20section%20of%20the,life%20forever%2C%20working%20alongside%20Jesus) - yet that hope also reshapes our work today.

## Your Calling: Work as God's Mission

Comer emphasizes that **every** job matters. Quoting _Colossians 3:23-24_, one blog notes "whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord" - because "all work is holy" by God's design[\[5\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=Colossians%203%3A23,are%20serving%20the%20Lord%20Christ)[\[6\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=All%20work%20is%20holy%20because,labourers%2C%20%C2%A0zoologists%2C%20explorers%2C%20architects%2C%20designers%E2%80%A6). We were not made to pull church and career apart. As a believer, you have a **dual vocation**[\[7\]](https://navmissionalenterprise.org/garden-city/#:~:text=,calling%20%E2%80%93%20to%20make%20disciples): one is your professional role, the other is your life mission. Comer vividly illustrates this for techies: _"If you're the IT guy…you have not one, but two callings"_[\[8\]](https://navmissionalenterprise.org/garden-city/#:~:text=,get%20to%20invite%20people%20to). First, be an excellent IT professional - "make your company's computer systems sing" as if recreating Eden[\[8\]](https://navmissionalenterprise.org/garden-city/#:~:text=,get%20to%20invite%20people%20to). Second, be a disciple - let your Christian character naturally raise questions about meaning, hope and joy[\[9\]](https://navmissionalenterprise.org/garden-city/#:~:text=again.%20Well%20done.%20,get%20to%20invite%20people%20to). In short, your day job **is** part of God's mission. Comer reminds us we're "image bearers…created to rule…to cooperate with God in building a civilization where his people can thrive"[\[3\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=You%E2%80%99ll%20hear%20a%20few%20John,a%20civilization%20where%20his%20people). This means writing code, designing networks or managing data should aim at "draw\[ing\] out the earth's potential and unleash it for human flourishing"[\[3\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=You%E2%80%99ll%20hear%20a%20few%20John,a%20civilization%20where%20his%20people).

There is **no sacred-secular divide** in God's economy. "God doesn't classify some jobs as holy and some jobs as not holy," one writer paraphrases Comer: "He's not into sacred and secular. All work done for God and to God can be holy"[\[10\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=for%20all%20the%20accountants%20out,there). Whether you're engineering software, baking servers, or crafting a product launch, you are stewarding God's creation. In practice, this might mean asking: _Does my project benefit the community? Does it make the world more 'garden-like'? Does it glorify God?_ Comer even suggests making these your job litmus tests[\[11\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=1,God%20has%20called%20us%20to). As Becky McKemey summarizes, God made each of us unique **"with individual passions and desires, which, when used for the glory of God in our work, bring us profound joy."**[\[12\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=God%20cares%20about%20what%20we,sole%20motivation%20behind%20our%20work). In other words, use your talents (coding skills, design sense, analytical mind) for God's glory and others' good.

## Identity: Image Bearers, Not Just Doers

Tech culture often pressures us to define ourselves by titles or output ("I'm a developer at X."). _Garden City_ pushes back: your primary identity is **"in God's image," not your job**[\[13\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=At%20the%20outset%2C%20he%20takes,we%20are%20created%20to%20be). As Comer shows, the lie of our culture is that "what we do defines who we are," but Scripture says the reverse: who we are (God's beloved children) gives meaning to what we do[\[13\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=At%20the%20outset%2C%20he%20takes,we%20are%20created%20to%20be). Becky notes how easy it is to compare jobs, pay, or status, but _Garden City_ reminds us that our worth comes from our Creator. This frees us from performance-driven anxiety. We follow the example of J.S. Bach, who signed all his music _Soli Deo Gloria_ - "to the glory of God." Our code, spreadsheets or customer service can carry the same motto.

Practically, this means you **live** your faith through excellence and integrity. Pastor Shaun Hays, who works in a secular warehouse, illustrates this: he avoids pushing religion at work ("most do not appreciate… discussions of religion so I avoid it"[\[14\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=It%20is%20well%20known%20in,my%20beliefs%20are%20sincere%2C%20since)), but he _does_ "go above and beyond the minimum" and "do \[his\] best all the time" on the job[\[15\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=I%20work%20in%20a%20secular,my%20best%20all%20the%20time). His hope is that colleagues will see Christ in his character ("I seek to please my Father… be the first person at work people point to for an example of what is best"[\[16\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=I%20seek%20to%20please%20my,both%20from%20time%20to%20time)). In short, let **who you are in Christ** guide how you work. This counters the tech-world idol of busyness and success, reminding us "we are valuable… because we are made in the image of God"[\[17\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=Work%20becomes%20an%20idol%20when,suffer%20exhaustion%2C%20anxiety%20and%20burnout), not by GitHub stars or salary.

## Rest and Resilience: Rediscovering Sabbath

The fast-paced tech world glorifies _hustle_: coding all-night, jumping from one sprint to the next. Comer and his readers offer a different rhythm: the **Sabbath**. Scripture shows God himself worked six days and "on the seventh day he rested"[\[18\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=God%20knows%20we%20need%20to,bearers%2C%20we%20should%20work%C2%A0and%C2%A0rest%20too), setting a pattern for us. _Garden City_ stresses: _we are not machines_. Burnout is real among tech workers, and Comer warns of its costs: "fatigue, burnout, anxiety, depression… as if God knew what he was saying when he commanded us to rest!"[\[19\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=The%20world%20is%20full%20of,he%20commanded%20us%20to%20rest). Rest isn't a 90-minute nap; it's a deliberate pause. Onelife's book club calls Sabbath "more than one day off - a day to enjoy the fruits of our labor, delight in life, and worship"[\[20\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=Sabbath%20rest%20is%20more%20than,his%20world%2C%20we%C2%A0celebrate%C2%A0life%2C%20we%C2%A0rest%C2%A0and%20we%C2%A0worship%C2%A0him).

This message even finds voice outside Christianity. Documentarian Tiffany Shlain coined the idea of a **"tech Sabbath"**. For over a decade she's observed one day a week without screens, and during the pandemic she reports it became _"ten times more important."_ Her children call it _"the only thing that hasn't changed"_[\[21\]](https://onbeing.org/programs/living-the-questions-weve-been-enmeshed-with-our-technologies-tech-shabbat-for-everyone/#:~:text=saying%2C%20%E2%80%9CFocus%20on%20what%20matters%2C,consistent%20thing%2C%20is%20this%20%E2%80%A6). She describes it as "an analog day of joy and family"[\[22\]](https://onbeing.org/programs/living-the-questions-weve-been-enmeshed-with-our-technologies-tech-shabbat-for-everyone/#:~:text=Shlain%3A%20%E2%80%A6%20this%20day%20that,of%20one%20day%20of%20rest) - an oasis of peace in a noisy week. We tech folks could use the same practice: set a rhythm to fully unplug at regular intervals. As Comer reminds us, Sabbath is a _signpost_ between this world and the next. By resting we acknowledge "God is God and we are not"[\[23\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=Besides%20the%20physical%2C%20emotional%20and,God%20and%20we%20are%20not) - we trust Him rather than our own productivity.

## Living in the Kingdom: Tech as Mission Field

Every day in the office is also a **missional** opportunity. Jeff Haanen (Translating Faith) points out that when we enter a workplace, **"various 'gods' and ultimate purposes are already there, and we are ultimately in a missionary context."**[\[24\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=organizations%2C%20institutions%2C%20markets%20or%20governments,ultimately%20in%20a%20missionary%20context). In plain terms, any company has an unspoken agenda (maximizing profit, innovation idol, consumerism, etc.). As Christians we enter with a Bible worldview. We're called to _"translate the Bible's story into the secular workplace,"_ demonstrating that Jesus' Lordship extends beyond church walls[\[25\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CThese%20ideas%20are%20fine%2C%E2%80%9D%20I%E2%80%99ve,based%20nonprofits)[\[26\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=Second%2C%20Christians%20often%20fear%20the,his%20faith%20at%20a%20Christian).

John Mark Comer invites us to think big: we are building a **Kingdom on earth**. He quotes Lesslie Newbigin to say companies under Christ's Lordship become the arena of God's powers (rather than worldly "principalities")[\[24\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=organizations%2C%20institutions%2C%20markets%20or%20governments,ultimately%20in%20a%20missionary%20context). One writer encapsulates Comer's vision: _"We're called to make a Garden-like world… where God's will is done 'on earth as it is in heaven.'… After all, we're just supposed to continue what God started in the beginning."_[\[27\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=To%20quote%20John%20Mark%20Comer,%E2%80%9D). Put simply, every ethical decision, helpful tool or faithful action in your tech role pushes back darkness and spreads the beauty of God's Kingdom.

## Faith at Work: Navigating a Secular Tech World

Tech companies today are rarely "openly religious." Many fall into Haanen's **faith-avoidant** or **faith-tolerant** categories[\[28\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=1.%20Faith,to%20accept%20Jesus%20as%20Lord)[\[29\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=2.%20Faith,into%20the%20work%20or%20company). In a faith-avoidant firm, talking about belief is "not appropriate here"[\[28\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=1.%20Faith,to%20accept%20Jesus%20as%20Lord); in a faith-tolerant one it's quietly allowed but never integrated (perhaps only through HR's diversity programs)[\[29\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=2.%20Faith,into%20the%20work%20or%20company). In practice, this means overt evangelism is often unwelcome. Instead, focus on other "callings" - excellence and kindness. **Live your faith softly**. As one tech pastor advises, avoid forced religious talk, but let your work ethic and integrity do the talking[\[14\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=It%20is%20well%20known%20in,my%20beliefs%20are%20sincere%2C%20since)[\[16\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=I%20seek%20to%20please%20my,both%20from%20time%20to%20time).

The book of Daniel offers a model: a believer in a pagan workplace, Daniel _"refused to worship the CEO"_ yet performed so well that "Daniel's religious views were broadcast throughout the corporation"[\[30\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=Should%20you%20find%20yourself%20in,Acts%2017%3A22). In other words, he was a faithful witness by refusing obvious compromises (dietary laws, idol worship) while excelling at his assignments. We can do likewise: refuse unethical practices, pray privately, and trust that God can use our integrity as testimony.

### Four Workplace "Postures" (David Miller's Framework)

- **Faith-Avoiding:** The company actively forbids religious talk. Leaders think "that's not appropriate here," and employees fear career consequences[\[28\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=1.%20Faith,to%20accept%20Jesus%20as%20Lord).
- **Faith-Tolerant:** Religion isn't banned, but it lives in the margins. Perhaps there's a prayer room or employee faith group, but church-talk is mostly private and occasional[\[28\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=1.%20Faith,to%20accept%20Jesus%20as%20Lord)[\[29\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=2.%20Faith,into%20the%20work%20or%20company).
- **Faith-Based:** (Rare in big tech) The founders' faith shapes the culture and operations openly.
- **Faith-Friendly:** A truly open culture where diverse beliefs are welcomed and discussed, though still with professionalism[\[29\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=2.%20Faith,into%20the%20work%20or%20company).

Most large tech firms are faith-avoidant or tolerant. The key is to be wise: if you're in a faith-avoidant context, focus on good work and discreet witness. If you're in a more tolerant place, you might even find space for a voluntary lunch Bible study or private prayer. In all cases, remember the mission: you "are serving the Lord Christ" in your work[\[5\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=Colossians%203%3A23,are%20serving%20the%20Lord%20Christ).

## **Applying _Garden City_ in Tech Life**

The ideas of _Garden City_ may seem lofty, but they translate into concrete steps:

- **Work Heartily:** Approach every task "as for the Lord, not for men"[\[5\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=Colossians%203%3A23,are%20serving%20the%20Lord%20Christ). Write clean code, meet deadlines, and serve users not just to impress bosses but to honor God. Remember Colossians' call to serve Christ in all we do.
- **Envision Impact:** Regularly ask Comer's questions[\[11\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=1,God%20has%20called%20us%20to): _Does my work help others flourish and make creation more "garden-like"? Does it glorify God? Am I living out my calling?_ These guideposts keep your projects kingdom-centered.
- **Rest Intentionally:** Institute a **tech Sabbath**. Even one screen-free day per week (Friday night to Saturday night, or another day) can radically recalibrate life[\[21\]](https://onbeing.org/programs/living-the-questions-weve-been-enmeshed-with-our-technologies-tech-shabbat-for-everyone/#:~:text=saying%2C%20%E2%80%9CFocus%20on%20what%20matters%2C,consistent%20thing%2C%20is%20this%20%E2%80%A6). Use it to recharge, pray, and enjoy family or creation.
- **Live Out Faith in Character:** You may not quote Scripture in Slack channels, but you can "be the person at work people point to for an example of what is best"[\[16\]](https://shaunhays.com/tag/work/#:~:text=I%20seek%20to%20please%20my,both%20from%20time%20to%20time). Show love to colleagues, admit mistakes, pray quietly in tough moments, and be consistent. Let others see that you value people above profit.
- **Engage Culture Creatively:** Look for ways technology can serve God's values: build inclusive software, use platforms for justice, or volunteer your skills for good causes. Remember Newbigin's idea: _imagine_ how your industry could be redeemed[\[31\]](https://jeffhaanen.com/translating-your-christian-convictions-for-a-secular-workplace/#:~:text=Ask%20yourself%3A%20What%E2%80%99s%20good%20about,do%20about%20it%20right%20now) (e.g. ask "What if all this tech-enabled communication was used to build community instead of just distraction?").
- **Keep Eternity in View:** _Garden City_ reminds us our work matters now **and forever**[\[4\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=The%20final%20section%20of%20the,life%20forever%2C%20working%20alongside%20Jesus). This liberates us from fear and success-addiction: we work because God is glorified, not merely to climb a corporate ladder. As Comer puts it, Sabbath and our tech efforts both say "God is God and we are not"[\[23\]](https://onelifeleaders.com/blog/young-adults-book-club-garden-city-review#:~:text=Besides%20the%20physical%2C%20emotional%20and,God%20and%20we%20are%20not), allowing us to trust His timing.

In sum, _Garden City_ paints a compelling vision: your tech job is _God's job_. By embracing a biblical view of work, rest and purpose, you can thrive spiritually in a secular setting. You'll gain a healthier perspective on work-life balance, and you'll find that code, chips and servers can all be part of God's much bigger garden. As Skye Jethani observes, Comer's book lets us see "our work and our world with new eyes"[\[32\]](https://www.zondervan.com/9780310337317/garden-city/#:~:text=that%20God%E2%80%99s%20mission%20includes%20all,com) - whether we're at a desk or on a deployment, we're building Heaven's city, one task at a time.

**Key Takeaways for Techies:** Every assignment is part of God's mission[\[3\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=You%E2%80%99ll%20hear%20a%20few%20John,a%20civilization%20where%20his%20people). Excel in your craft as worship[\[5\]](https://onepassiononedevotion.wordpress.com/2024/11/19/made-to-work/#:~:text=Colossians%203%3A23,are%20serving%20the%20Lord%20Christ), rest as God commands[\[21\]](https://onbeing.org/programs/living-the-questions-weve-been-enmeshed-with-our-technologies-tech-shabbat-for-everyone/#:~:text=saying%2C%20%E2%80%9CFocus%20on%20what%20matters%2C,consistent%20thing%2C%20is%20this%20%E2%80%A6), and let your innovation and love testify to a better Kingdom.
